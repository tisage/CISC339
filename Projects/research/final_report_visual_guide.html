Required questions

What a real answer looks like

These pairs aren't full sample paragraphs — copying a full example is exactly the shortcut this assignment is designed to catch. Instead, see the difference in kind between an answer that restates the abstract and one that shows you actually understood it.

Q2 · UNDERSTANDING THE METHOD

“What is the paper's main method, in your own words?”

Too generic

The paper proposes a novel framework that leverages large language models to generate and detect spam, achieving strong performance on the benchmark dataset.

Why it's weak: this is just the abstract's vocabulary rearranged. No one can tell from this whether you read past the abstract.

Shows understanding

They basically put an LLM on offense and defense at once: one prompt role writes spam designed to dodge known filters, then a separate detector role (sometimes the same model, sometimes a different one) has to catch it. The twist is they measure whether a detector trained only on human-written spam still works once the spam is LLM-written.

Why it's strong: specific mechanism, in plain language, and names the actual comparison the paper is making.

Q5 · YOUR REACTION

“What's one thing you found surprising, confusing, or disagreed with?”

Too generic

I found this paper very interesting and it taught me a lot about how LLMs can be used for security purposes.

Why it's weak: a generic compliment. Could be copy-pasted onto any paper about any topic.

Shows understanding

I was surprised the detector's accuracy barely dropped against LLM-generated spam — I expected synthetic spam to be much harder to catch. I'd push back on treating that as reassuring, though, since their generator was the same era of model as off-the-shelf tools; a model fine-tuned specifically to evade filters might tell a different story.

Why it's strong: a specific, falsifiable reaction, plus a reason — this is a claim you could actually defend if asked.

AI-use policy

Where the line is

Use AI the way you'd use it for any other assignment this term — as a research assistant, not a ghostwriter.

OK to do

  • Find and summarize candidate related papers before you pick your 5
  • Explain a confusing term or method in simpler language
  • Give feedback on a draft you already wrote
  • Help format citations correctly

Not OK to do

  • Copy-pasting AI-generated paragraphs as your answers
  • Asking AI to write the report and submitting it with minor edits
  • Citing a paper you never actually looked at
ResearchGate

Posting your report, step by step

These are schematic walkthroughs of the upload flow — not screenshots — since the exact layout can change. The buttons and fields you're looking for will be in roughly these places.

1

Create your account and go to your profile. A school email works fine. Fill in your name, institution, and major.

2

From the dropdown, choose “Research” (not Project or Question) — that's the upload type for a document.

3

Upload your PDF and set the publication type to “Preprint” or “Technical Report.” Not “Article” — this isn't peer-reviewed.

4

Copy the publication URL. This is the link you submit alongside your PDF — the whole flow takes about 10–15 minutes.

Grading

What earns the grade

Understanding (Q1–3)
Problem, method, and findings explained accurately in your own words — not the abstract restated.
Citations
6+ total, one consistent style, every source genuinely relevant.
Reflection (Q4–5)
A specific, real reaction — something you could defend if someone pushed back on it.
Forward-looking (Q6)
A concrete open problem or next step, not “more research is needed.”
Format
2–3 pages, proofread, organized.
Before you submit

Submission checklist

  • Report is 2–3 pages, PDF, one citation style used throughout
  • 6+ citations total, with a formatted reference list at the end
  • All six required questions answered somewhere in the report
  • Report uploaded to ResearchGate as Preprint / Technical Report
  • PDF + ResearchGate link both submitted on Blackboard
Questions about whether a source counts as “related enough,” or anything else here — ask on the discussion board or in office hours. This page is a visual companion to the full written guide; the written guide is the one with binding detail if the two ever disagree.
